How to Calculate Rebar Weight and Cutting Length

Updated 5 October 2026 · العربية

Two numbers decide every steel order: how much a bar weighs, and how long it must be cut. This page gives the formula for the first, the principle for the second, and a worked example.

Weight of a reinforcing bar

Steel weighs about 7850 kg per cubic metre, so the weight per metre of a round bar of diameter d (in millimetres) is:

weight (kg per metre) ≈ d² ÷ 162

The table below uses d² ÷ 162.2, which matches the unit weights printed in most steel catalogues.

Worked example

20 bars of 16 mm, each cut to 6.5 m:

  • Weight per metre of 16 mm = 16² ÷ 162.2 = 1.578 kg/m
  • Weight of one bar = 1.578 × 6.5 = 10.26 kg
  • Weight of 20 bars = 10.26 × 20 = 205.2 kg

Always add the weights of every bar mark per diameter: steel is bought and priced by diameter and length.

Cutting length

The cutting length is the length of the straight bar before it is bent. For a straight bar it is simply its length. For a bent bar it is the sum of the straight parts of the shape, corrected at every bend and at every hook:

  • Dimensions on drawings and in bar schedules are usually measured to the outside of the bar, so the sum of the dimensions is longer than the steel really is. A deduction is made at each bend; its size depends on the bar diameter and the bend angle.
  • Hooks and bar ends add an allowance that also depends on the diameter.
  • The exact deductions and allowances are different in BS 8666, ACI and other standards, so always use the formula of the standard your project follows.

Unit mistakes to avoid

  • Diameter in millimetres, length in metres: the formula above gives kg per metre.
  • Do not mix millimetre schedules with feet-inch drawings; convert once and write it down.
  • Check the total in tonnes against a rule of thumb for the member type before the order goes out.
